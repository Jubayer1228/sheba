# What it takes to win pharmacy delivery in Bangladesh

Research synthesis for Sheba (ULAB/Future Startup white paper, Arogga/MedEasy coverage, telemedicine UX studies, adoption research).

## The hard truth

Bangladesh already has a pharmacy on almost every street. People go there for **speed**, **pharmacist advice**, and **habit**. Pure “order medicine online, get it tomorrow” loses to walking 5 minutes—unless Sheba is clearly better on dimensions retail cannot match.

## Non-negotiable wedges

### 1. Hyperlocal speed beats next-day ecommerce
Most competitors advertise same-day / next-day. Sick customers need **30–60 minutes**. Sheba’s model (order from a *nearby* partner pharmacy, not a central warehouse) is the right architecture. Product must scream ETA, distance, and “ready for pickup in 15 min.”

### 2. Trust is the #1 adoption blocker
Counterfeit / low-quality meds are a real fear (estimates often cite ~20–30% of circulating product at risk). Users also distrust ecommerce after bad experiences.

Bake into the product:
- Named neighborhood pharmacy on every order (not anonymous warehouse)
- Sealed / batch / “partner verified” cues
- Pharmacist confirmation on Rx
- Clear returns / wrong-medicine policy
- COD still first-class

### 3. Never ask for bKash PIN
BD users reject apps that request MFS PINs. Prefer: pay in bKash/Nagad app → paste **Transaction ID**, or official gateway redirect. COD remains default for first orders.

### 4. Prescriptions + chronic care, not only OTC
- Photo Rx upload is table stakes
- Family profiles (order for parents/children)
- Refill reminders for diabetes, BP, thyroid (aging population → chronic load rising hard by 2050)
- “Call pharmacist” when handwriting is unclear

### 5. Omnichannel > pure-play online
White paper: trust accrues faster when digital sits on real shops people already know (Lazz, Square counters, Ibn Sina). Sheba should market as **the network of pharmacies you already trust**, not a replacement for them.

### 6. Compete where retail is weak
| Retail wins | Sheba should win |
|-------------|------------------|
| Instant cough syrup at midnight walk | Compare price/stock across 5 nearby shops |
| Pharmacist chat at the counter | Chat/call + Rx verification without leaving home |
| One shop stockouts | Multi-pharmacy fulfillment |
| No history | Family med history + refill calendar |
| Cash only friction for elders | Family member pays via bKash for parents |

### 7. Low-connectivity & Bangla
Usability studies of Arogga/ePharma/MedEasy stress **slow network resilience** and literacy diversity. Ship Bangla UI, light pages, offline-friendly order status, large tap targets on the *mobile app*—web can stay dense and powerful for desktop users (caregivers, office workers ordering for home).

## Out-of-the-box bets (differentiation)

1. **“Someone is sick” mode** — one-tap: upload Rx → auto-pick nearest open Rx pharmacy → COD → live ETA. Fewer screens than Foodpanda dinner.
2. **Price radar** — same SKU (e.g. Seclo 20mg) priced across nearby partners; user picks shop by fee+ETA+price.
3. **Office → home relay** — Gulshan/Motijheel workers order for Dhanmondi parents (dual-address family wallet).
4. **Night & emergency layer** — filter 24/7 / late-night partners (Farmgate-type); surcharge OK if honest.
5. **Sealed authenticity trail** — photo of sealed pack + invoice from partner pharmacy attached to order (anti-counterfeit theater that becomes real ops).
6. **Pharmacist-assisted cart** — user uploads Rx only; pharmacist builds cart; user approves total (huge for elders / messy handwriting).
7. **Mosque/clinic micro-hubs** later — pickup lockers near diagnostic centers after Dhaka density is proven.

## What not to do early

- Don’t become full-stack telemedicine + labs on day one (trust dilution; white paper warns premature expansion).
- Don’t hide which pharmacy fulfills the order.
- Don’t force prepaid digital wallet for first purchase.

## Sheba web positioning (this redesign)

Desktop web = caregivers, office workers, power users comparing pharmacies. Mobile app (separate) = on-the-go / “I’m sick now.” Web UI should feel like a **modern health commerce console**, not a phone chrome with bottom tabs.
